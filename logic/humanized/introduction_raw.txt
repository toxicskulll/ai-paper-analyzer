**Introduction**

The advent of high-frequency data and advanced computational capabilities has sparked an ongoing quest to refine and improve models used in stock market analysis and prediction. This research aims to contribute to this field by critically examining the existing body of literature on stock market modeling and forecasting, with a specific focus on statistical models, machine learning techniques, and hybrid approaches. By investigating the limitations of these methodologies and proposing potential solutions, this study aspires to provide valuable insights into enhancing the accuracy, generalizability, and applicability of stock market prediction models.

Several papers have addressed the issue of stock market volatility analysis using statistical models such as the GARCH model (e.g., [Paper A]). While these studies have demonstrated promising results in volatile market conditions, they are hampered by limitations like restricted sample sizes and limited generalizability to other markets or regions. To address these shortcomings, future work could explore the applicability of these approaches during crisis periods and investigate their potential impact on diverse stock markets worldwide.

Similarly, studies employing machine learning techniques, such as support vector machines (SVMs) for volatile stock market prediction (e.g., [Paper C]), have shown promise in volatile market conditions. However, the narrow focus on volatile market conditions and limited sample sizes limit the applicability of these approaches to other market conditions. To expand the scope of machine learning applications in this domain, future research should investigate the potential of these techniques in stable markets and assess their effectiveness in combination with other machine learning methods or statistical models.

Hybrid approaches that combine statistical models and machine learning techniques have also shown promise for improving stock market prediction accuracy (e.g., [Paper D]). However, like other methodologies, hybrid models face limitations such as restricted sample sizes and limited generalizability to other markets. To address these issues, future research should investigate the applicability of these approaches in diverse market conditions, such as crisis periods, and expand their application to various stock markets worldwide.

In summary, this research aims to build upon existing studies in stock market modeling and forecasting by exploring potential improvements for increased accuracy, generalizability, and applicability. By refining current models, investigating their applicability across different market conditions, developing hybrid models that leverage the strengths of multiple approaches, and comparing results with other prediction methods, this study aspires to contribute valuable insights into the most suitable approach for stock price forecasting during volatile periods.
